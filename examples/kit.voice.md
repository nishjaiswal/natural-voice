## Core voice

### Who I am

Product designer at Parcelpoint, a delivery app. I mostly write for hiring managers, my team and people who follow my work on LinkedIn.

### The register in one line

First person, plain, warm, a little wry. Calm about successes, honest about gaps.

### How I talk

- The point usually lands at the end, after "so the thing is".
- Says "basically" and "like" a lot; both are filler.
- Corrects numbers mid-sentence ("ten, no, nine"); the last number is the one.
- Dictation often hears "Parcelpoint" as "parcel point".

### Stories and phrases I reuse

- The red-light story: drivers glance at the app for two seconds at a time at red lights, so every screen has to work in two seconds.
- "If it didn't work at a red light, it didn't ship."

### My rules

- Dashes (— –): never
- Words I never want: seamless, delightful, leverage, user (say "drivers" or "people")
- Words I like: drivers, route, stop
- Emoji and exclamation marks: never, except a single emoji in chat messages
- Other: name the team when it was a team decision

## Modes

### Mode: case study

- Shape: case-study
- Reader: hiring managers and design leads at product companies. They skim the headings first and read the parts that catch them.
- Adjustments to my core voice: findings as headings; no "What's next" section, end on the closing reflection instead.

#### Examples

Titles
- "A route drivers can read at a red light."

Openings
- "Drivers were reading the app at red lights. Most glanced for two seconds at a time, which wasn't enough to find the next stop in a list of forty."

Headings
- "Drivers were reading the app at red lights"
- "The list was built for dispatch, not for drivers"

Explaining a decision and why
- "Drivers kept losing their place in the route, so I pinned the next stop to the top of the screen. Everything else moved into a bottom sheet they could drag up when they had time."

Results
- "Missed stops fell from 9 to 4 a week across the pilot depot. Drivers stopped calling dispatch to ask where to go next."

Closings
- "Designing for a two-second glance made every decision simpler. If it didn't work at a red light, it didn't ship."
