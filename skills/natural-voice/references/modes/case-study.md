# Mode: storytelling case study

For portfolio case studies (design, product, research, engineering). The piece tells a story about a problem, the decisions made, and what happened.

## Story spine

Where the person gave them, each section and the whole piece move through **setup, tension, decision, result**; don't invent a tension or a result to complete the pattern. Lead with the strongest real thing the person has. Use a result if one exists with a clear origin; otherwise start with the problem.

## Typical order

1. **Title**: one short statement, not a label. "A form people actually finish" works; "Form redesign" doesn't.
2. **Summary**: 2 to 4 sentences covering what the product is, what the person did, and the headline result with its origin.
3. **Facts block**: role, timeline, team, platform, status.
4. **Opening paragraph**: the problem, told plainly.
5. **What was wrong**: what the person saw, and how they saw it.
6. **Decisions**: one heading per decision. Each heading is a statement: either a finding ("Most people left before typing anything") or a principle ("Show the work before asking for it"). Each decision is tied to an image where there is one.
7. **Before and after**: where the person shows an old and a new version, with a "What changed" line.
8. **Final screens or artefacts**: each with a one- or two-sentence caption that says what it shows and, if they said so, why it matters.
9. **Results**: numbers with origins, then what they meant for people.
10. **What the work shows, and what's next**: honest limits and next steps (only from what they said; otherwise `[ADD: ...]`, or leave it out).
11. **Closing**: one short reflection (only from what they said; otherwise `[ADD: ...]`, or leave it out).

Length: 800 to 2,000 words. Strong case studies are shorter than people expect.

## Headings

Statements, in sentence case. Never phase labels such as "Research", "Process", "Solution" or "Conclusion", unless the person's samples use them.

## Captions

One or two sentences about what is on screen and, if they said so, why it matters. Write them in the present tense. Don't open with "This screen shows".
