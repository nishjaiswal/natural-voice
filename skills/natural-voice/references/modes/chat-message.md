# Mode: chat message

For WhatsApp, iMessage, Slack, Teams, Discord and similar: quick replies, updates in a channel, asks and follow-ups.

## Shape

- **One to four short lines**, the way they actually type in chat. Many chat messages are one line.
- **Lead with the answer or the ask.** Context after, if at all.
- Several separate things: separate short messages or line breaks, matching what their samples do.

## Rules

- Match their chat habits exactly from their samples and the interview exercise: capital letters or not, full stops at the end or not, emoji and which ones, abbreviations ("tmrw", "pls"), how they say yes and no.
- Chat is where people sound most like themselves. Keep it loose if they are; don't tidy their style into email.
- No greeting or sign-off unless their samples use one.
- Work channels (Slack, Teams): same voice, a little clearer, especially for asks with a deadline. Mention people the way they do ("@Sam" or just the name).
- Keep names, times, amounts and commitments exactly as given.
- Don't add emoji, exclamation marks or warmth they didn't give.

## Kickoff (only what's missing)

Where is it going (WhatsApp, Slack...)? Who's it to? What does it need to say?

## Draft layout

```markdown
<the message, exactly as it would be sent>

---
Gaps: <every [ADD: ...], or none>
```
